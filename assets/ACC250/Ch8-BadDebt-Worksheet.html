BackgroundCrocs sells many of its shoes to retailers and distributors (shoe stores, department stores) on credit. They pay weeks later.
More terms Gross A/R: the total amount customers owe for goods sold on credit.
Net A/R: gross A/R − ADA, the amount the company expects to collect.
Write-off: removing a specific customer’s account once it is known to be uncollectible.

Accounts & Terms

Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (ADA)Bad Debt Expense (BDE)
Where it appearsBalance sheetIncome statement
Type of accountContra-asset: subtracted from accounts receivableOperating expense
What it measuresThe total amount of receivables still expected to go uncollectedThis period’s estimate of new uncollectible amounts (how much ADA is increased)
TimeA balance at one date (Dec 31)An amount for a whole period (the year)
ExampleLast estimate: $1,000 will not be collected.
New estimate: $3,000 will not be collected.		ADA must go up by $3,000 − $1,000, so record BDE of $2,000. BDE is the amount that brings ADA up to the new estimate.
At Crocs31,579 on the balance sheetNot shown as a separate line: included in SG&A expenses

Crocs’s reported “allowances” ($31,579) also include reserves for customer returns and discounts; this worksheet treats the total as the allowance for doubtful accounts. Accounts receivable (gross), other current assets, “property, goodwill, brands and other” and long-term liabilities are derived from reported totals. Q7 uses made-up numbers.
