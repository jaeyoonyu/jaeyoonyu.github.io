Part 3 · How the accounts connect (Inventories were 54,892 on Jan 31, 2024.)

  1. Inventory flow. Beginning inventory + Purchases − Cost of sales = Ending inventory. How much inventory did Walmart buy during the year?   Purchases = I
  2. Gross margin % = Gross profit ÷ Net sales = J   Out of every $100 of sales, about how many dollars are left after paying for the goods? K
  3. Inventory turnover = Cost of sales ÷ Average inventory = L
    Days an item sits on the shelf ≈ 365 ÷ L = M
  4. Who pays for the shelves? Accounts payable (58,666) is larger than inventories (56,435). In one sentence, what does that tell you?
    Walmart usually sells the goods before it has to pay its suppliers, so suppliers are financing Walmart’s inventory. (It also explains why current liabilities exceed current assets.)

Part 4 · Concepts

  1. LIFO. Walmart’s U.S. stores use LIFO. When purchase costs are rising, LIFO (vs. FIFO) gives: Cost of sales higher/lower; Net income higher/lower; Ending inventory higher/lower.
    LIFO puts the newest (most expensive) costs into cost of sales, and leaves the oldest (cheapest) costs in ending inventory.
  2. Membership fees. On Nov 1, a customer pays $50 cash for a 12-month Sam’s Club membership. On Walmart’s Dec 31 books: revenue earned = N; still owed as a liability = O, called P.
    $50 × 2/12 = $8.33 is earned (Nov and Dec). The other $41.67 is unearned (deferred) revenue until the service is provided. (Ch4 adjusting entry.)
