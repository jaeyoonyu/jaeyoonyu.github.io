Key concepts (hints)

Income statement

Revenue (net sales)
What the company earns from selling goods and services, recorded when earned.
Cost of sales (cost of goods sold)
The cost of the inventory that was sold during the period. It leaves the balance sheet and becomes an expense.
Gross profit
Net sales − cost of sales.
Operating expenses (SG&A)
Costs of running the business other than the goods themselves: wages, rent, utilities, advertising, depreciation.
Operating income
Revenues − cost of sales − operating expenses. Profit from the main business, before interest and taxes.
Income tax expense (provision)
The government’s share of the profit.
Net income
What is left after all expenses, interest and taxes.

Balance sheet

Assets = Liabilities + Equity
Everything the company owns is financed by creditors or by owners.
Current assets / current liabilities
Expected to be used up or paid within one year.
Accounts receivable
Amounts customers owe the company, expected to be collected in cash.
Inventory
Goods held for sale, recorded at cost until sold.
Accounts payable
Amounts the company owes suppliers for goods or services bought on credit.
Retained earnings
Total net income earned since the start, minus dividends paid to shareholders.

Finally, calculate (use the numbers on the front)

9Gross profit margin
= (Net sales − Cost of sales) ÷ Net sales
10Net profit margin
= Net income attributable to Walmart ÷ Total revenues
11Current ratio
= Total current assets ÷ Total current liabilities
9: 162,785 ÷ 674,538 = 24.1%  ·  10: 19,436 ÷ 680,985 = 2.9%  ·  11: 79,458 ÷ 96,584 = 0.82.
Walmart keeps only about 3 cents of every sales dollar as profit. A current ratio below 1 is normal for Walmart: it sells its inventory before it has to pay its suppliers (accounts payable is larger than inventory).

Some lines are combined. “Other equity, net” = accumulated other comprehensive loss (13,605) + noncontrolling interest 6,408. “Long-term debt, leases and other” includes a $271M redeemable noncontrolling interest.
