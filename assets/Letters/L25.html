Back to the Letter
  • Buffett (1979–1981): with inflation above 10%, good-looking returns were “illusory” for owners.
  • Historical cost: reliable, but it can mislead when prices change fast.

Questions
  1. A company earns a 12% return on equity while inflation is 10%. Roughly what is the real (inflation-adjusted) return, in %?
  2. Why do historical-cost financial statements overstate earnings when prices rise?
    1. Revenue is recorded at old prices
    2. Assets are marked to market
    3. Taxes are ignored
    4. Depreciation and cost of goods sold are based on older, lower prices
