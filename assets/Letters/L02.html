Step 1: Start with Operating Cash Flow
  • Net cash from operating activities (CFO): cash from running the business (customers in, suppliers and employees out).
  • Found in the first section of the statement of cash flows.

EX. Amazon 2002: net cash provided by operating activities = $174 million.

Step 2: Subtract Capital Expenditures
  • Capital expenditures (capex) = purchases of fixed assets (PPE), in the investing section.
  • Free cash flow = CFO − capex: cash left after keeping and growing the asset base.

Back to the Letter
  • Bezos (2004): free cash flow per share is Amazon’s “ultimate financial measure.”
  • Why not earnings? Earnings do not directly become cash: working capital, capex and dilution also matter.

Questions
  1. In 2002 Amazon’s net cash provided by operating activities was $174 million and its purchases of fixed assets were $39 million (2002 letter). What was its free cash flow ($ millions)?
  2. Why does Bezos focus on free cash flow rather than earnings?
    1. Free cash flow ignores capital spending
    2. Earnings do not directly turn into cash: capital spending, working capital and new shares also matter
    3. Earnings are not audited
    4. Free cash flow is required by GAAP to be the headline number
